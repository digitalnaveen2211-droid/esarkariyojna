Government Schemes in India, Explained Simply

Every year the Government of India and the state governments run hundreds of welfare schemes for farmers, students, women, senior citizens, workers and small businesses. These schemes aim to provide financial support, affordable healthcare, housing, scholarships, employment and social security to the people who need them most. Yet many eligible people never benefit, simply because they do not have clear information. Rules are often written in complicated language, the application process can be confusing, and some people end up on fake websites or paying middlemen for help they could get for free.

e-Sarkari Yojna is a private, informational portal built to solve this problem. We explain central and state government schemes in plain language, so you can decide for yourself which scheme fits your situation, which documents you need, and where and how to apply.

Schemes for Farmers

For farming families, the government offers income support, crop insurance, Kisan Credit Cards, irrigation support and subsidies on seeds and fertilisers. Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samman Nidhi (PM-KISAN) and Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana are two of the best-known examples across the country.

Scholarships and Education Schemes for Students

School and college students can apply for many central and state scholarships, including pre-matric, post-matric and merit-based awards. Most scholarships are applied for through the National Scholarship Portal or the relevant state scholarship portal.

Schemes for Women and Girls

There are schemes for a girl child's education and savings, support for pregnant women, and loans and livelihood programmes for women's self-help groups. Savings schemes such as Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana help families plan for their daughters' future.

Health and Insurance Schemes

Health insurance schemes such as Ayushman Bharat – Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana help poor and vulnerable families get free or affordable hospital treatment. Low-premium life and accident insurance schemes are also available to most bank account holders.

Housing, Pension and Employment Schemes

We also explain Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana for affordable housing, pension schemes for old-age security, and skill development and self-employment schemes for young people, all in easy-to-follow language.

Central Schemes vs State Schemes

Central schemes are funded and designed by the Government of India and are usually available in every state, sometimes with a share of the cost paid by the state. State schemes are launched by individual state governments, so their names, benefits and eligibility rules can differ from one state to another. When you read a scheme page on e-Sarkari Yojna, check whether it is a central or a state scheme, and if it is a state scheme, make sure it applies to the state where you live. Your domicile or residence certificate is often the deciding document for state schemes.

How to Choose the Right Scheme

Follow these steps before applying for any scheme. They save time and reduce the chance of your application being rejected:

  1. Identify your need: decide what kind of help you are looking for, such as education, healthcare, housing, farming or employment.
  2. Use categories or search: type a scheme name or keyword in the search box above, for example "farmer", "scholarship" or "pension".
  3. Read the eligibility carefully: check the age, income and residence conditions. If you are not eligible, avoid applying.
  4. Keep your documents ready: have copies of your Aadhaar, bank passbook, photographs and certificates ready in advance, and make sure your bank account is linked to Aadhaar.
  5. Apply only on the official website: use the official link on each scheme page and save your application or registration number.

Stay Safe While Applying Online

Fraud in the name of government schemes is unfortunately common. Keep these points in mind to protect yourself:

  • Genuine government scheme websites usually end in .gov.in or .nic.in. Always check the website address before applying.
  • Never share your OTP, bank password or ATM PIN with anyone over a call, message or in person.
  • Applying for most government schemes is free. Be careful if someone asks for money to "guarantee" a benefit.
  • For help, contact your nearest Common Service Centre (CSC), gram panchayat, block office or the concerned department.
